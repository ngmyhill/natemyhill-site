01 · The Idea
Surgery is rarely the only option, and never a decision made alone.
For Melburnians weighing up shoulder, upper-limb or joint surgery, Dr Nate Myhill is the orthopaedic surgeon who builds the plan with you, not for you. Fellowship-trained and plainly spoken, he treats every patient as a collaborator, weighing the options against your values, your goals, and the life you want to get back to.

It’s a quieter kind of confidence: no hype, no pressure, no jargon left unexplained. Just a clear-headed surgeon and a plan that actually fits.
Who it’s for
GPs and patients across Melbourne and the north-east, weighing up surgery anywhere from the shoulder to the knee.
The category
General orthopaedic surgery, with a special interest in the shoulder, upper limb and sports injuries.
The difference
Shared decision-making, practiced deliberately. Every choice is yours to make, and the plan is built with you, never for you.
02 · The Three Pillars
Three promises every word, image and decision answers to.
Pillar One
We decide together
Shared decision-making is the practice, not a slogan. You have a say in every choice, and every plan is built around your values, goals and lifestyle. You’re always a participant, never a passenger.
Pillar Two
We get you moving
Trained across the whole of orthopaedics, with a special interest in the shoulder, upper limb and sports. One surgeon for the whole limb, so a GP never has to wonder where to send you. Surgery is never the point in itself: it is in service of getting you moving, and back to the life you want.
Pillar Three
We say it straight
Plain English, every time. Clear correspondence for your GP, honest options for you, and the jargon explained before it’s ever used. Considered, Australian, never reliant on hype.
03 · How We Sound
Warm, but never chummy. Plain, but never dumbed-down. Certain, but never superior.
01
Talk to one person
Always “you”, never “patients”. One person, one conversation, across the desk.
02
Lead with the decision
Open with the choice and the options, not the name of the procedure.
03
Explain, don’t impress
Drop the jargon, or define it the first time. Clever is not the goal. Clear is.
04
Calm and certain
No hype, no fear, no superlatives. Confidence reads quietest when it’s real.
Off-brand, and off-limits
“Dr Myhill is a leading shoulder surgeon offering world-class rotator cuff repair with excellent outcomes.”
Comparative, self-congratulating, and a breach of the AHPRA advertising rules. It also tells the patient nothing they can use.
On-brand
“Your rotator cuff is the group of muscles that steady your shoulder. If it’s torn, we’ll talk through whether surgery is the right call, and what life looks like either way.”
Speaks to one person, explains the jargon, and leads with the decision. Compliant by design.
04 · The Mark
An arm finding its arc again.
The mark is a single sweep: the path of a limb returning to motion. It is deliberately left open: that gap is the decision made together, the room for the patient to step in. The point at its leading edge is where the sweep is headed: forward, and back to a full life.
